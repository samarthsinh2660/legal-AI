Someone is occupying my land without my permission for the last 5 years. Can I file a suit for eviction under the Specific Relief Act? What are the key elements I need to prove?

Assembling the answer

Yes, but not under Section 6. Because the occupation has lasted five years, the six-month window for a summary possession suit has closed — you must sue on the strength of your title under Section 5 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963, read with Article 65 of the Limitation Act.1

Applicable Law

Section 5, Specific Relief Act, 1963: Recovery of specific immovable property. A person entitled to the possession of specific immovable property may recover it in the manner provided by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.1

Note: Section 6 (suit by a person dispossessed without consent) is not available here, as it requires filing within six months of dispossession.2

Key elements to prove
  • Title. A registered instrument establishing ownership — sale deed, gift deed, or succession document — with an unbroken chain.
  • Identity of the property. Survey number, boundaries and area matching the title document and revenue record.
  • Better title than the defendant. In a Section 5 suit you succeed on the strength of your own title, not the weakness of theirs.
  • Suit within limitation. Twelve years from when the defendant's possession became adverse to you.3
Key judgments
  • Ravinder Kaur Grewal v. Manjit Kaur, (2019) 8 SCC 729 — a plaintiff may seek a declaration of title based on adverse possession; possession-based rights can be asserted as a sword, not only a shield.1
  • Rame Gowda v. M. Varadappa Naidu, (2004) 1 SCC 769 — settled possession is protected even against the true owner, except through due process of law.2
  • Nair Service Society v. K.C. Alexander, AIR 1968 SC 1165 — possession is good title against everyone except the rightful owner.3
What may need further verification
  • The exact date the occupation began — this determines whether the twelve-year limitation has run.
  • Whether the occupier has asserted a competing claim of adverse possession on record.
  • Whether any civil suit over the same survey number is already pending.

Patel v. Shah

Suit for declaration of title and permanent injunction regarding commercial property situated in Ahmedabad. The core issue turns on the interpretation of the 1998 sale deed.

AI Case Summary

Verified

The plaintiff (Patel) asserts ownership based on a registered sale deed from 1998, while the defendant (Shah) claims adverse possession and challenges the validity of the original deed, citing procedural irregularities during registration. The court has granted an interim injunction maintaining the status quo. The primary legal burden rests on the plaintiff to establish unencumbered title under the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.

Key finding: The analysis identifies a high probability of the court focusing on the limitation period for challenging the 1998 deed, with State of Gujarat v. Desai (2015) as the most relevant precedent.
