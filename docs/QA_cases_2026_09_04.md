# QA — Cases and document upload, live deploy, 2026-09-04

Manual pass against `https://legal-ai-swart-one.vercel.app`, signed in as an
existing account. Real case, real document, a five-turn conversation. No
code changed during this pass.

**Test matter:** "Verma v. Malhotra — Cheque Dishonour", NI Act s.138,
State: Delhi, Court left blank. Context: *"Cheque for Rs. 5,00,000 issued by
the respondent towards repayment of a loan was dishonoured for insufficient
funds. A statutory demand notice was sent on 2 August 2026 and no payment
was received within 15 days."* Document uploaded: a realistic s.138 demand
notice (`demand_notice.txt`) naming a cheque number, both banks, and three
specific dates (issue, dishonour, notice).

---

## What works

- **Case creation, listing, and the workspace page** — all correct. State
  recorded, court correctly left blank (shown as "No court on record"
  rather than a guessed value).
- **Document upload** — the `.txt` file uploaded, was accepted, and shows
  in the Documents list labelled "YOUR DOCUMENT".
- **Jurisdiction inheritance genuinely works.** Asking the first question
  in this case fired *no* clarification question, even though the same
  question asked bare (outside a case) would normally ask "Which state is
  this in?" for a state-dependent matter type. Recording the state once on
  the case and having every thread skip re-asking is the one case-context
  benefit I could fully confirm end to end.
- **The case banner is correct and persistent** — every turn in the thread
  showed *"Part of Verma v. Malhotra — Cheque Dishonour — every answer here
  starts from the matter's context"* linking back to the case.
- **Legal reasoning quality, where it engaged at all, was strong.** Turns 1
  and 3 correctly distinguished the summary s.6 Specific Relief Act suit
  (6-month bar) from title-based limitation, and correctly cited s.138's
  proviso (c), s.142's one-month filing window, and the 2003 amendment that
  raised the penalty from one to two years — all with real S.C.R. citations.

## What does not work — three distinct bugs, one thread, five turns

### 1. The case's own Context field is not reaching the analyst

Turn 1 asked *"Has the 15-day notice period expired, and can my client file
a complaint?"* — a question the case's own Context field already answers
(it names the notice date and says 15 days passed with no payment). The
answer was:

> *"The retrieved materials do not contain the specific dates or facts of
> your client's case to determine whether the 15-day notice period has
> expired."*

The statutory framework in the same answer was correct and well-cited. What
was missing was specifically the case's own stated facts — despite the UI's
own promise, twice over, that "every answer here starts from the matter's
context" and the case page's own description: *"This seeds every research
thread attached to the case — it is context for the agents, not a private
note."* On this evidence, for date/fact questions, it does not.

**Confirmed a second time, in a second, unrelated case — and worse.**
Prompted directly by the user asking whether case data cross-contaminates
between cases, a second case was built specifically to test that:
"Sharma v. Kapoor — Rent Arrears", Karnataka, ₹3,60,000 in rent arrears
owed by a named tenant to a named landlord, no document attached. Asked
*"What is the total amount in dispute in this matter, who owes it, and to
whom?"* — again a question the case's own Context answers directly. The
answer:

> *"a total sum of Rs. 2,92,57,559/- ordered to be refunded by Respondent
> No. 1 to the appellant in Odisha State Financial Corporation v. Vigyan
> Chemical Industries and Others..."*

A real judgment, about an unrelated bank-guarantee dispute, with the wrong
amount, the wrong parties, and nothing to do with rent — presented as if
it answered "the amount in dispute in this matter." This is a worse
failure mode than case 1's honest *"the materials do not contain..."*:
here the same underlying gap (case Context not reaching the analyst) was
filled by an unrelated retrieved citation dressed up as case-relevant,
which a reader skimming the answer could easily mistake for their own
case's figures.

**What this test also confirms, positively:** no fact from case 1 (Verma,
Malhotra, ₹5,00,000, the cheque) appeared anywhere in case 2's answer, or
vice versa when re-checked. Case data does **not** cross-contaminate
between cases — that isolation held under a direct, deliberate test built
to break it. The bug is that a case's own facts are not reliably reaching
the analyst that answers its own threads — not that one case's facts leak
into another's.

### 2. A same-thread, same-topic follow-up returned "no such provisions exist"

Turn 2, in the *same thread that had just correctly cited s.138 three
times*, restated the dates directly in the question: *"The demand notice
was sent and received on 2 August 2026. Has the 15-day period expired as of
today?"* The answer:

> *"The provided legal authorities do not contain any provisions or
> information regarding Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, the
> computation of the 15-day notice period, or demand notices sent under
> that Act."*

This is not a coverage gap — the same corpus answered the same statute
correctly one turn earlier (turn 1) and one turn later (turn 3, below).
Something about this specific phrasing — a question that leads with date
arithmetic rather than a legal question — appears to break retrieval
entirely for that one turn, most likely in how the follow-up gets rewritten
into a search query. Not root-caused within this QA pass (no code was read
or changed); server logs carried no detail beyond the request/response
lines, which is itself worth noting (see "worth fixing" below).

**Reproduction:** thread `f344f4c177de4ef38465217ad76b2781`, exactly the
two questions quoted above, in that order, in a case-attached thread.

### 3. A question about the case's own uploaded document is refused as out of scope

Turn 4: *"What is the cheque number and which bank was it drawn on, per the
uploaded demand notice?"* — a plain factual lookup against a document the
case itself holds. The answer:

> *"I only research Indian law — statutes and judgments — so I cannot help
> with that. Ask a legal question and I will search the corpus and show
> you what the answer rests on."*

The out-of-scope refusal exists to stop the planner inventing a legal angle
for "write me a poem" (see `docs/SPEED_2026_09_03.md`/session notes on that
fix). Here it fired on a question that is squarely what a case with an
attached document exists to answer. A lawyer using this case workspace
would ask exactly this question and be told the product cannot help.

### Recovery, for the record

Turn 3, back to a plainly-phrased legal question ("What is the punishment
under Section 138...?"), answered correctly with full citations — so the
thread was not permanently broken, only turns 2 and 4 specifically.

## Minor: no way to open or remove an uploaded document

Clicking the uploaded document's row in the case workspace does nothing —
no preview, no delete option, no menu. Once a document is attached, the UI
gives no way to check what it actually is or to remove it. Not tested via
the API directly, so this may exist server-side with no frontend for it;
recorded as observed UI behaviour only.

## Worth fixing, not urgent

Server logs for the failed turn (bug #2) carried no more detail than the
request/response access line — no warning, no trace of what the rewritten
question or search query actually was. Diagnosing bug #2 properly, or any
similar case in future, needs that logged.

## Answering the actual question asked: does a case make answers *better* than plain research?

**Confirmed better, once:** jurisdiction is recorded on the case and every
thread skips the "which state" clarification. That is real and it works.

**Not shown to be better, and shown to actively regress, twice:** a plain
research thread with no case attached has never been observed (this
session or prior ones) to refuse a document-content question, because a
plain thread has no document to be asked about — the failure mode is
specific to cases. And the date-arithmetic retrieval failure (bug #2) is a
general thread bug, not case-specific, but it was hit here, in a case
thread, as part of normal case-driven conversation.

The promise a case workspace makes — "this seeds every thread, it is
context for the agents" — is not currently delivered for the two things a
lawyer would actually use a case for: recalling facts already on file, and
reading back their own uploaded document. Jurisdiction is the one part of
that promise that holds.

**One question explicitly checked and confirmed correct: cases do not leak
into each other.** A second, unrelated case (different parties, amount,
matter type, state, no document) was built specifically to test this, and
neither case's facts appeared in the other's answers under direct
questioning. Whatever is wrong with cases right now is a case not reliably
being read from — not one case's data being handed to another's.

## Root cause and fix, 2026-09-04

### Bug #1 — fixed and verified

`context.serialization.render()` includes `case_description`, but `render()`
is called only from the `research` graph node (the planner). The `analyst`
node — the one that actually writes the answer — called `analyse()` with
`documents=` but never `case_description=`, and `_render_case()` had no
slot for it. The planner saw the matter; the writer never did.

Fixed in `legal_ai/agents/analyst.py` (`_render_case` and `analyse` both
take `case_description`) and `legal_ai/graph/nodes/__init__.py`'s
`analyst()` node (now passes `context.case_description` through). Covered
by `tests/agents/test_analyst.py::test_the_case_description_reaches_the_prompt`.

### Bug #3 — fixed and verified

`_permitted_documents` (`api/threads/controller.py`) returned `[]` whenever
no `document_ids` were explicitly requested, rather than defaulting to the
case's own stored files. The live chat composer has no per-message attach
control on a case thread, so `document_ids` is empty on every case-thread
message — meaning the `document` graph node never ran and a case's own
uploaded document was never readable, ever. `stream_message` (the actual
SSE path the UI uses) additionally never called `_permitted_documents` at
all, using the raw, unfiltered request body value.

Fixed: `_permitted_documents` now defaults to `list_case_files(conn,
case_id)` when `requested` is empty and a case is attached; `stream_message`
now calls it instead of using `document_ids` raw. Covered by
`tests/api/threads/test_controller.py::test_no_requested_documents_defaults_to_the_whole_case`.

### Bug #2 — partially fixed; deeper cause identified, not resolved

Root cause was **not** the rewriter, contrary to the original hypothesis.
Reproduced live against the real model:

- `rewrite_question()` correctly resolved the turn-2 follow-up, carrying
  "Section 138" forward from turn 1's history.
- `plan_research()`'s single model call, however, sometimes substituted the
  wrong figure into its search query — one run produced "...demand notice
  within **thirty** days of receipt" for a question that explicitly said
  fifteen. That wrong number, not a routing failure, is what missed
  `act:2189:sec-138` in retrieval.

**Fixed:** `research_plan.py`'s `PROMPT` now explicitly instructs the
planner to carry over every number in the question exactly rather than
substituting from memory. Verified live, 3 runs post-fix: the query now
says "15 days" every time; the hallucination is gone.

**The real cause, traced stage by stage.** An earlier draft of this section
blamed the umbrella Act document (`act:2189`) for crowding the section out.
That was wrong, and measurement is what disproved it: junk act documents
take 2 of 500 top-10 slots across the benchmark (0.4%), while s.138 sat at
rank 75 — freeing one slot could not have reached it.

Where it actually goes, for "Under Section 138 ... has the 15-day notice
period expired as of today":

    signal          planner's angle query     the user's own question
    keyword                     -                         -
    vector                      -                         -
    metadata                    -                    rank 1
    after RRF                   -                    rank 1
    after rerank                -                    rank 43
    after fusing both passes                         rank 75

Two defects, both measured:

1. `search_metadata` resolves the reference exactly, and both later stages
   throw it away. RRF damps a lone signal's rank-1 vote by design, so two
   signals agreeing on a near-miss outscore it; the cross-encoder then
   scores the section's text against a question about *dates* rather than
   substance and demotes it to 43. Turn 3 ("what is the punishment under
   s.138") survived only because that section's text reads like an answer
   to that question — the lookup was right both times, the reranker only
   agreed once.

2. `extract_section_references` needs "Section N of the X Act" word order.
   The planner rewrites into statutory keyword vocabulary ("Negotiable
   Instruments Act 1881 section 138 demand notice 15 days"), which the
   parser cannot read at all — so on that pass the exact lookup is silent.
   It survived only because the second pass carries the reader's wording.

**Fix: `_exact_first` in `retrieval/hybrid.py`.** A resolved reference is
ground truth, not a candidate, so it is pinned ahead of the ranked list
rather than ranked against it. Resolved for both phrasings, so the
planner's rewrite can no longer silence it.

Measured on `evals/datasets/retrieval_citations.json`, `--fused` (the
shipped path), 19 questions:

    MRR        0.326  ->  0.921
    recall@1     11%  ->    89%
    recall@10    58%  ->    95%
    misses      8/19  ->   1/19

The reproduction above goes from miss to rank 1. The 50-question benchmark
cannot see this change at all: 0 of its 50 questions name a section, which
is why the defect survived this long — the eval set had no coverage of the
path. That is the blind spot `retrieval_citations.json` now fills.

**A second defect the new eval exposed.** The reference pattern matched
only statutes ending in "Act", so the Indian Penal Code (575 sections),
Code of Criminal Procedure (525), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (282) and
Code of Civil Procedure (171) could not be named — 1,553 sectioned
documents unreachable by exact lookup, and most of Indian criminal and
procedural practice. The pattern now takes both shapes ("... Penal Code"
and "Code of Criminal Procedure").

**Still open, deliberately: the junk act documents.** 845 of 863
`document_type='act'` rows hold the India Code page's navigation chrome
("Screen Reader Access ... Browse Central Acts ...") rather than statutory
text, each carrying its Act's real title. A known scraping failure mode,
but measured at 2/500 top-10 slots it is an ingestion cleanup worth doing
on its own account, not a retrieval fix.
